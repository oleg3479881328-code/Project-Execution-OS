# Marriage-Based I-485 After USCIS PM-602-0199

Type: `workflow-lesson`
Lifecycle status: `candidate`
Captured: 2026-06-06
Review status: researched and preserved; fast-changing area requiring recheck before reuse

## Problem

USCIS issued Policy Memorandum PM-602-0199 on May 21, 2026. Public messaging, later media clarification, attorney commentary, and reported field practice created uncertainty about how adjustment-of-status applications are being treated, including marriage-based Form I-485 cases.

## Reusable Lesson

For a marriage-based I-485 matter after PM-602-0199, do not rely only on the traditional eligibility checklist.

Use two separate layers:

```text
statutory eligibility
  -> discretionary preparation
  -> current field-practice check
  -> deadline and travel-risk review
  -> attorney-escalation decision
```

Marriage to a U.S. citizen remains legally important because a spouse is an immediate relative under 8 U.S.C. § 1151(b)(2)(A)(i). Adjustment remains discretionary under 8 U.S.C. § 1255(a). Certain bars in § 1255(c)(2) do not apply to immediate relatives, but this does not eliminate admissibility, fraud or misrepresentation, criminal, removal-history, entry-method, travel, or discretionary risks.

## Current Practical Pattern

Until clearer USCIS or court guidance exists:

- assume heightened discretionary scrutiny may occur;
- update bona fide marriage evidence;
- prepare a positive-equities package;
- prepare a truthful, reviewed answer to why adjustment of status is appropriate in the specific case;
- reconcile entry, status, work, travel, and filing timelines;
- do not withdraw, depart, or switch procedures reflexively;
- obtain individualized attorney review for adverse facts or high-risk procedural decisions.

## Evidence Grades

Keep separate:

1. statutes and regulations;
2. official USCIS material;
3. professional attorney interpretation;
4. reliable reporting;
5. forums and anecdotal signals.

Do not treat a forum report as law or a media clarification as a replacement for an official memo.

- any USCIS clarification or injunction.

## Escalation Boundary

Attorney review is required before recommending a procedural step when any of these may apply:

- ESTA or Visa Waiver Program entry;
- contemplated departure after unlawful presence;
- fraud or misrepresentation concern;
- criminal history;
- removal history;
- prior inconsistent statements;
- denial, NOID, material RFE, or adversarial interview;
- switching to consular processing;
- travel while I-485 is pending.
