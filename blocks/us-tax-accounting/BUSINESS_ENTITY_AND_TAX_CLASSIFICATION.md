# Business Entity and Tax Classification

## Purpose

Separate legal entity choice from federal, state, and local tax treatment.

## Core Rule

An LLC is a legal structure, not a complete tax answer. Confirm both legal form and tax classification.

## Common Structures

Review as applicable:

- individual / sole proprietorship;
- single-member LLC;
- multi-member LLC;
- partnership;
- C corporation;
- S corporation election;
- nonprofit or exempt organization;
- disregarded entity;
- rental, investment, or passive-activity structure;
- foreign-owned or cross-border structure.

## Questions To Resolve

Confirm:

- legal owner or owners;
- formation state;
- operating states;
- effective dates;
- federal tax classification;
- elections filed or considered;
- ownership percentages;
- worker and payroll plans;
- expected income and expenses;
- liability and contract needs;
- financing and investor plans;
- state franchise, annual-report, or minimum-tax obligations;
- local registration requirements.

## Decision Boundary

Do not recommend a structure solely to reduce taxes. Consider:

- legal liability;
- administrative burden;
- payroll requirements;
- bookkeeping complexity;
- filing costs;
- state and local obligations;
- investor requirements;
- timing and election deadlines;
- professional review.

## Escalation

Use a CPA, EA, or tax attorney when:

- an S corporation election is being evaluated or changed;
- ownership is shared or changing;
- multiple states are involved;
- foreign ownership or foreign activity exists;
- prior elections may be late or defective;
- the entity is being converted, merged, dissolved, or sold;
- tax savings claims depend on aggressive assumptions;
- legal liability, contracts, or securities issues are material.

## Output

Produce:

- legal-entity summary;
- tax-classification summary;
- jurisdiction list;
- open questions;
- election and filing checklist;
- professional-review recommendation.
