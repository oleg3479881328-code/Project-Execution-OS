# Federal, State, and Local Routing

## Purpose

Prevent federal-only answers when state and local obligations may exist.

## Core Rule

For every taxpayer or project, build a jurisdiction matrix before making a compliance recommendation.

## Jurisdiction Inputs

Record:

- residence state;
- formation state;
- states where work is performed;
- states where employees or contractors work;
- states where inventory is stored;
- states where products are shipped;
- states where services are delivered;
- marketplaces and payment channels;
- physical locations;
- local municipalities;
- remote-worker locations;
- prior registrations;
- notices received.

## Federal Review

Check as applicable:

- income tax;
- self-employment tax;
- payroll tax;
- information returns;
- EIN and entity classification;
- excise tax;
- foreign reporting;
- retirement-plan or benefit-plan obligations;
- credits and deductions.

## State Review

Check as applicable:

- state income tax;
- withholding;
- unemployment tax;
- sales and use tax;
- franchise tax;
- gross-receipts tax;
- annual report;
- entity registration;
- marketplace rules;
- remote-worker rules;
- state-specific credits or deductions.

## Local Review

Check as applicable:

- municipal income tax;
- city business registration;
- local payroll withholding;
- county or city sales tax;
- local business-license tax;
- occupational tax;
- local filing portals.

## Nexus Rule

Do not make a nexus conclusion from a generic checklist. Verify the current law and official guidance for the specific state, activity, period, and tax type.

## Marketplace Rule

Do not assume a marketplace eliminates all sales-tax duties. Confirm what the marketplace collects, what the seller must still register for or file, and whether other channels create obligations.

## Output

Produce a matrix with:

- jurisdiction;
- tax or registration type;
- triggering facts;
- official source;
- current status;
- deadline;
- open question;
- professional escalation if needed.
