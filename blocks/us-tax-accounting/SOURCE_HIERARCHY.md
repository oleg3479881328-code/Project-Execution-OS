# US Tax and Accounting Source Hierarchy

## Purpose

Use the strongest available source for the exact question. Tax and accounting answers become unsafe when agents rely on stale summaries, search snippets, or generic memory.

## Source Order

1. Current federal primary sources
   - Internal Revenue Code and Treasury regulations when legal interpretation is material
   - current IRS forms, instructions, publications, notices, revenue procedures, FAQs, and official topic pages
   - SSA sources for W-2 and W-3 workflows
   - FinCEN sources for FBAR and beneficial-ownership questions
   - Department of Labor sources when worker classification or wage rules overlap
2. Current state primary sources
   - state revenue departments
   - state secretary of state
   - state labor and unemployment agencies
   - state sales-tax portals and registration guidance
3. Current local primary sources
   - city, county, municipal, and local tax-authority pages
4. Current official portals
   - official registration, filing, payment, and account portals
5. Professional secondary sources
   - CPA, EA, payroll-provider, or accounting-platform explanations only after primary-source verification
6. Community content
   - use only for discovery or practical experience; never treat it as authority

## Verification Rules

Before answering a current question, confirm:

- tax year;
- filing period;
- effective date;
- form version;
- jurisdiction;
- taxpayer type;
- entity type;
- tax classification;
- activity type;
- whether the source is current;
- whether a state or local rule changes the result.

## Use of IRS Publications

IRS publications are practical official guidance, but they are not substitutes for the Code, regulations, binding guidance, or case-specific professional review when the issue is disputed or high risk.

## Search-Snippet Rule

A search result snippet is not evidence. Open the source, read the relevant page, confirm the date, and preserve the URL and retrieval date.

## Annual Snapshot Rule

Store stable workflow rules in permanent files. Put annual thresholds, rates, mileage amounts, standard-deduction figures, contribution limits, due dates affected by weekends or holidays, form changes, and temporary relief in dated research artifacts or current-snapshot files.

## State and Local Rule

Never treat state and local tax as footnotes. For each business or side-income workflow, determine whether state income tax, municipal income tax, franchise tax, gross-receipts tax, sales tax, unemployment tax, payroll withholding, registration, or local filing obligations may apply.

## Legal Escalation

Route legal interpretation, controversy, notices, appeals, disputes, privilege questions, and material audit exposure through `blocks/us-law/BLOCK.md` and a qualified professional when required.
