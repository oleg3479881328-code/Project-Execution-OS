# Tax and Accounting Intake

## Purpose

Collect the minimum facts needed before giving tax or accounting guidance.

## Intake Rule

Do not jump to deductions, forms, or software before the facts are clear.

## Core Intake

Record:

- taxpayer name or project identifier;
- tax year or filing period;
- filing status when relevant;
- state of residence;
- states and cities where work is performed;
- entity legal name;
- entity type;
- tax classification;
- EIN status;
- business start date;
- accounting method if known;
- bank accounts and payment channels;
- income streams;
- products and services;
- sales channels and marketplaces;
- contractors;
- employees;
- payroll provider if any;
- major asset purchases;
- vehicle use;
- home-office use;
- inventory if any;
- existing bookkeeping system;
- last reconciled month;
- known notices or overdue filings;
- current deadlines;
- professional already involved.

## Document Checklist

Collect only into an approved secure project layer:

- prior returns;
- EIN letter;
- entity formation documents;
- bank and credit-card statements;
- payment-processor exports;
- marketplace reports;
- payroll reports;
- contractor list and W-9 forms;
- 1099 forms received and issued;
- invoices and receipts;
- mileage log;
- asset-purchase records;
- sales-tax registrations and returns;
- tax notices;
- bookkeeping exports;
- loan and financing records.

## First Classification

Assign one initial workflow:

1. individual side income;
2. gig economy;
3. solo founder;
4. small service business;
5. ecommerce or digital products;
6. employer with payroll;
7. cleanup and catch-up bookkeeping;
8. year-end handoff;
9. notice triage;
10. multi-state or local-tax review.

## Stop Conditions

Stop and escalate before substantive recommendations when:

- the entity or tax classification is unclear;
- the deadline may already have passed;
- a notice has a response date;
- books are materially incomplete;
- payroll may have been run incorrectly;
- workers may be misclassified;
- sales-tax nexus may exist in multiple states;
- large unreported income or prior-year errors are possible;
- foreign accounts, foreign ownership, or international activity are present;
- criminal, fraud, privilege, or litigation issues may exist.
