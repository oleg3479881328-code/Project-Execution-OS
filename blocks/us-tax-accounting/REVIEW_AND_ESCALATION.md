# Review and Escalation

## Purpose

Provide a quality-control layer for tax and accounting outputs.

## Review Checklist

Before treating an answer or workflow as ready, verify:

- the tax year and filing period are explicit;
- the taxpayer and entity are identified;
- legal entity and tax classification are not confused;
- federal, state, and local jurisdictions were reviewed;
- current primary sources were opened;
- source dates and retrieval dates are recorded;
- deadlines were verified;
- books quality is stated;
- unresolved facts are visible;
- assumptions are separated from verified facts;
- worker-classification questions are not oversimplified;
- sales-tax questions are routed by state, activity, and channel;
- confidential records are kept outside the reusable block;
- the correct professional role is named when escalation is needed.

## Escalation Matrix

### Bookkeeper

Use for routine capture, categorization, reconciliation, and cleanup.

### Accountant or CPA

Use for accounting treatment, close quality, entity analysis, planning, and complex reporting.

### EA

Use for federal tax representation and IRS-focused matters where appropriate.

### Payroll Specialist

Use for payroll setup, deposits, returns, corrections, and multi-state payroll operations.

### Sales-Tax Specialist

Use for multi-state sales-tax exposure, product taxability, registration strategy, and retroactive review.

### Tax Attorney

Use for legal interpretation, disputes, notices with material exposure, appeals, collection risk, audit defense, and privilege-sensitive matters.

## Red Flags

Escalate immediately when:

- a notice has a response date;
- payroll deposits may be missing;
- tax was collected but not remitted;
- prior-year returns may be wrong or missing;
- books are materially incomplete;
- worker classification is disputed;
- foreign accounts, ownership, or transactions exist;
- multiple states create unresolved exposure;
- criminal, fraud, or litigation concerns may exist;
- the agent cannot verify the controlling source.

## Final Rule

An organized answer is not the same as a professionally reviewed answer. State the boundary clearly.
