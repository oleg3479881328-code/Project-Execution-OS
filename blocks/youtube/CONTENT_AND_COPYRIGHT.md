# Content and Copyright

## Purpose

Define originality, copyright, reuse, and transformation rules for YouTube channel planning.

## Core Rule

YouTube monetization and channel durability depend on original value, not merely technical editing.

Do not confuse:

- transformed content;
- reused content;
- copyright permission;
- monetization eligibility.

They are related but not identical.

## Original Value

Strong original-value signals:

- original script;
- original commentary;
- analysis;
- education;
- criticism;
- narrative structure;
- unique editing purpose;
- meaningful transformation;
- original voiceover;
- original visual assets where possible.

## Reused-Content Risk

High-risk patterns:

- clip compilations with little commentary;
- reposts;
- lightly edited clips;
- automated slideshows;
- repetitive template videos with little added value;
- text-to-speech videos without meaningful editorial contribution;
- mass-produced near-duplicates.

## Copyright Review

Before publication, record:

- source;
- rights assumption;
- whether source is owned, licensed, public-domain, Creative Commons, or used under a defensible transformative rationale;
- music rights;
- visual rights;
- voice/avatar rights;
- whether a Content ID claim or strike risk exists.

## Transformation Review

Ask:

- What new value did we add?
- Is the viewer watching for our explanation or for the borrowed clip itself?
- Is the borrowed material limited to what the new purpose needs?
- Is the final video meaningfully different from the source?
- Can the channel survive review if a human examiner watches several videos?

## AI Content Rule

AI-assisted content is not automatically bad.

But low-effort, repetitive, mass-produced content is risky.

Review:

- originality;
- factual accuracy;
- repetition;
- human editorial value;
- disclosure when required;
- identity/impersonation risk.

## Final Rule

Build channels around original editorial value. Do not rely on superficial edits to make reused material safe or monetizable.
