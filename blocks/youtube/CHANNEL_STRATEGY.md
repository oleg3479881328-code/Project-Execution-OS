# YouTube Channel Strategy

## Purpose

Define reusable channel-strategy rules for YouTube projects.

## Core Rule

A channel must have one clear audience, one recognizable value promise, and a repeatable content system.

## Channel Definition

Before launch, define:

- target audience;
- niche;
- channel promise;
- primary content format;
- supporting content format;
- publishing cadence;
- monetization path;
- conversion path outside YouTube when relevant.

## Niche Rule

Prefer a narrow initial niche over a broad mixed channel.

Good examples:

- gadget discoveries;
- cinema facts;
- English phrases from movies;
- AI tools for creators;
- product comparisons;
- educational micro-lessons.

Avoid mixing unrelated audiences on one new channel.

## Content Pillars

Use 2 to 4 repeatable content pillars.

Example for gadget channel:

- unusual gadgets;
- product tests;
- comparisons;
- useful tools under a price point.

Example for QuizLight / language channel:

- phrase from movie;
- pronunciation;
- context;
- memory card demo.

## Format Ladder

Define:

- discovery format;
- trust format;
- conversion format.

Typical pattern:

- Shorts for discovery;
- long-form videos for trust;
- product demo, affiliate link, or lead magnet for conversion.

## Channel Naming

A good channel name should be:

- clear enough to understand;
- broad enough to allow growth;
- memorable;
- usable across languages when scaling;
- consistent with niche and monetization.

## Channel Launch Minimum

Prepare before launch:

- channel name;
- profile image;
- banner;
- bio/about text;
- link strategy;
- 3 to 10 initial video ideas;
- first repeatable template;
- analytics log.

## Review Questions

Ask:

- Is the audience obvious?
- Is the niche narrow enough?
- Can this format be repeated 30 times?
- Is there a business path?
- Is the originality/value-add clear?
- Can the owner produce it consistently?

## Final Rule

Do not start a YouTube channel from isolated video ideas. Start from a repeatable channel system.
