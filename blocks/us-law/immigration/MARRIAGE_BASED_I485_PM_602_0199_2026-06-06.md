# Marriage-Based I-485 And USCIS PM-602-0199

Date checked: 2026-06-06
Status: current research note; `candidate`

## Date Clarification

This note describes the situation as of June 6, 2026.

USCIS Policy Memorandum PM-602-0199 was issued on May 21, 2026. It did not exist on June 6, 2024.

## Scope

This note focuses on Form I-485 adjustment-of-status applications based on marriage to a United States citizen.

It is a research summary, not individualized legal advice.

## Confirmed Statutory Baseline

A spouse of a United States citizen is an `immediate relative` under 8 U.S.C. § 1151(b)(2)(A)(i). Immediate relatives are not subject to the ordinary numerical visa limitations.

Adjustment of status remains discretionary under 8 U.S.C. § 1255(a): an eligible applicant who was inspected and admitted or paroled may be adjusted in the discretion of the government if statutory requirements are satisfied.

Certain status and unauthorized-employment bars in 8 U.S.C. § 1255(c)(2) do not apply to immediate relatives. That does not erase other requirements or risks, including admission or parole, admissibility, fraud or misrepresentation, criminal issues, removal history, and discretionary review.

## What The Memo Appears To Change

Based on the official USCIS release URL, major reporting, attorney commentary, and reported field practice:

- the memo does not repeal the adjustment-of-status statute;
- the memo does not create a published blanket statutory ban on marriage-based I-485 cases;
- it emphasizes that adjustment is discretionary;
- the initial public framing treats adjustment inside the United States as appropriate only in extraordinary circumstances;
- later DHS media clarification reportedly described the memo as a reminder of discretionary authority rather than a blanket elimination of adjustment;
- no follow-up memo resolving the ambiguity was located during this research pass;
- field implementation appears uneven.

## Reported Field Practice

Reported operational changes include:

- officers asking why the applicant used adjustment of status rather than consular processing;
- questions about whether anything prevented the applicant from returning to the home country;
- questions about family remaining abroad;
- some approvals without discussion of the memo;
- some decisions reportedly held while officers await clarity;
- reported RFEs seeking evidence of positive equities such as taxes, employment, family ties, community involvement, and roots in the United States.

Treat these as reported practice, not binding law.

## Practical Effect For Marriage-Based I-485 Cases

Marriage to a U.S. citizen remains legally important, but it should not be treated as an automatic shield against discretionary scrutiny.

A pending or planned marriage-based case should be reviewed for:

- manner of entry;
- status timeline;
- intent at entry, especially for B-1/B-2, F-1, ESTA, and other nonimmigrant entries;
- overstay or unauthorized employment;
- prior applications and statements;
- prior marriages and petitions;
- criminal history, including arrests;
- fraud or misrepresentation risk;
- removal history;
- bona fide marriage evidence;
- hardship and family-unity facts;
- positive equities;
- travel plans;
- consular-processing risks.

## Attorney Recommendations Reported In Current Sources

Common recommendations include:

- do not withdraw a pending I-485 reflexively;
- do not depart the United States or switch to consular processing without individualized legal review;
- prepare a positive-equities package;
- update bona fide marriage evidence;
- prepare to answer why adjustment of status is appropriate in the specific case;
- respond carefully and comprehensively to any RFE;
- obtain attorney review for adverse facts or ambiguous entry history.

Potential positive-equities evidence:

- tax returns;
- lawful employment and economic contribution;
- employer letters;
- educational credentials;
- lease or mortgage;
- utilities and bank records;
- U.S.-citizen or lawful-permanent-resident family ties;
- children and school records;
- community, volunteer, charitable, or religious involvement;
- medical or humanitarian facts;
- proof of a stable household;
- clean immigration and criminal record when accurate.

## Forums And Community Signals

Reddit and other community discussions show widespread concern among applicants with marriage-based I-485 cases, including applicants who entered on student, visitor, employment, DACA-related, or fiancé pathways.

A self-identified immigration attorney and former USCIS worker hosted a Reddit discussion about the memo and anticipated RFEs and scrutiny for pending cases. This is an operational signal only. It is not official guidance and the identity or advice should not be treated as independently verified authority.

Community discussion suggests K-1 fiancé cases may be factually different because that path contemplates marriage and later adjustment after a consular process. That remains an unverified forum interpretation unless USCIS or counsel confirms it for a specific case.

## Cases And Litigation Status

As of June 6, 2026, this research pass did not locate a published court decision or clearly indexed complaint specifically deciding the legality of PM-602-0199.

This does not prove that no new or non-indexed filing exists in PACER.

Separate litigation has challenged other USCIS policy changes, but those cases should not be confused with a PM-602-0199-specific challenge.

## Immediate Safe Rule

For a pending marriage-based I-485:

- do not withdraw;
- do not leave the United States;
- do not switch to consular processing;
- do not submit a casual explanation of prior intent or immigration history;

without individualized immigration-attorney review.
