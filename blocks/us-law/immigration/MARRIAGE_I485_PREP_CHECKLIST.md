# Marriage-Based I-485 Preparation Checklist

Date: 2026-06-06
Status: candidate checklist

## Purpose

Prepare a marriage-based Form I-485 case for attorney review, interview preparation, or an RFE response after USCIS PM-602-0199.

This is a preparation checklist, not legal advice.

## 1. Case Posture

Record:

- U.S.-citizen spouse or lawful-permanent-resident spouse;
- I-130 filing date and status;
- I-485 filing date and status;
- I-765 and I-131 filing status;
- interview notice, RFE, NOID, denial, or other USCIS notice;
- field office;
- upcoming deadlines;
- attorney representation status.

## 2. Entry And Immigration Timeline

Prepare a complete timeline:

- each U.S. entry and exit;
- visa or parole category for each entry;
- I-94 records;
- status expiration dates;
- periods of overstay;
- work authorization dates;
- unauthorized employment, if any;
- prior extensions, changes of status, petitions, applications, refusals, and interviews;
- advance-parole travel;
- ESTA or Visa Waiver Program entry;
- removal, detention, voluntary departure, or immigration-court history.

Do not guess. Reconcile the timeline with documents.

## 3. Intent And Consistency Review

Flag for attorney review:

- marriage, engagement, or wedding planning before entry;
- statements made at a consular interview, airport, border, or USCIS interview;
- visitor or student entry followed quickly by marriage or filing;
- inconsistent addresses, employment history, dates, or prior answers;
- social-media posts, messages, or documents that may conflict with prior statements;
- prior immigration filings or petitions;
- prior marriages or sponsorships.

Do not improvise explanations about intent or prior statements.

## 4. Bona Fide Marriage Evidence

Update evidence of a genuine shared life:

- marriage certificate;
- joint lease, deed, or mortgage;
- joint utilities;
- joint bank or credit accounts;
- insurance policies;
- tax filings;
- shared vehicles or major purchases;
- photographs across time and with family;
- travel records;
- correspondence and communications when appropriate;
- children’s records;
- affidavits where useful;
- evidence explaining any periods of living apart.

## 5. Positive Equities Package

Prepare evidence that may support favorable discretion:

- tax returns and tax transcripts;
- lawful employment records;
- employer letters;
- educational credentials;
- professional licenses;
- economic contribution;
- stable household records;
- family ties in the United States;
- children and school records;
- medical or caregiving responsibilities;
- community involvement;
- volunteer, charitable, or religious activity;
- letters of support;
- absence of adverse history when accurate.

## 6. Consular-Processing Hardship Facts

Prepare facts, not conclusions:

- effect of separation on spouse and children;
- caregiving obligations;
- medical needs;
- employment disruption;
- financial hardship;
- country conditions relevant to the individual;
- inability to travel safely;
- visa-processing uncertainty;
- unlawful-presence bar risk after departure;
- loss of work authorization or travel document;
- other family-unity consequences.

Attorney review is required before deciding whether to depart or switch procedures.

## 7. Interview Preparation

Prepare truthful, consistent answers to:

- why adjustment of status was used rather than consular processing;
- what the applicant intended at the last entry;
- when the relationship became serious;
- when marriage was discussed or planned;
- whether anything prevents return abroad;
- family ties abroad and in the United States;
- status, employment, and travel history;
- prior applications and statements;
- shared life with the spouse.

Do not memorize a false story. Do not volunteer speculative explanations.

## 8. RFE Response Package

If USCIS issues an RFE:

- preserve the full notice, envelope, and deadline;
- identify every requested item;
- compare the request with prior filings;
- collect evidence in an indexed package;
- explain gaps with documents where possible;
- obtain attorney review before submitting a discretionary narrative;
- submit by the exact deadline and preserve proof of delivery.

## 9. Mandatory Attorney Review Triggers

Obtain individualized immigration-attorney review for:

- ESTA or Visa Waiver Program entry;
- unlawful presence and contemplated travel;
- unauthorized employment;
- prior removal, court, detention, or ICE history;
- criminal arrest or charge;
- fraud or misrepresentation concern;
- visitor or student entry with pre-existing immigration plan;
- prior inconsistent statements;
- prior marriage-based petition;
- denial, NOID, RFE with material issues, or adversarial interview;
- switching to consular processing;
- travel while I-485 is pending;
- any uncertainty about whether a statutory exception applies.

## 10. Evidence Preservation

Store securely:

- complete filing package;
- USCIS receipts and notices;
- delivery proof;
- attorney notes;
- immigration timeline;
- interview preparation notes;
- evidence index;
- source links and date checked;
- unresolved risks.

## Final Rule

Prepare documents early, answer truthfully, and do not make a high-risk procedural decision without individualized legal review.
