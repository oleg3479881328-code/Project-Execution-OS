# US Immigration Law Block

## Purpose

This sub-block gives `Project Execution OS` a reusable workflow for researching United States immigration-law issues with current sources, clear evidence grading, deadline triage, and attorney escalation.

## Status

`candidate`

## When To Use

Use this sub-block when work involves:

- adjustment of status and Form I-485;
- family-based immigration;
- marriage-based green-card cases;
- USCIS forms, interviews, RFEs, NOIDs, denials, or policy memoranda;
- consular processing;
- unlawful presence, admissibility, fraud or misrepresentation risk;
- advance parole, employment authorization, travel, or status questions;
- removal proceedings, detention, or immigration-court issues;
- immigration-law monitoring.

## Core Rule

Do not answer an immigration question from memory alone.

Use:

`facts -> immigration history -> dates -> status and entry method -> petitioner relationship -> filing posture -> deadline and travel-risk check -> statute and regulation -> current USCIS policy -> agency instructions -> case-law or administrative precedent -> reported field practice -> attorney escalation -> evidence package`

## Evidence Grades

Separate sources into:

1. primary law: statutes, regulations, official instructions, court opinions, BIA decisions;
2. official agency material: USCIS, DHS, DOS, EOIR, CBP, ICE pages, policy manuals, memoranda, forms, and alerts;
3. professional interpretation: immigration attorneys, AILA materials, law-firm alerts;
4. reliable reporting: major news organizations quoting officials and attorneys;
5. operational signals: forums, Reddit, community groups, and anecdotal field reports.

Do not treat operational signals as law.

## Official Source Baseline

Use the relevant current official sources:

- INA and codified immigration statutes;
- Title 8 CFR;
- USCIS Policy Manual;
- USCIS form pages and instructions;
- Department of State Visa Bulletin and Foreign Affairs Manual when applicable;
- EOIR and BIA materials when removal proceedings or administrative precedent matter;
- CBP and ICE materials when entry, parole, detention, or enforcement matters;
- federal court decisions when litigation matters.

## Attorney Escalation

Escalate promptly when any of these may apply:

- removal proceedings, detention, prior removal, deportation, or voluntary departure;
- unlawful presence with contemplated departure;
- fraud, misrepresentation, inconsistent forms, or credibility risk;
- arrest, charge, conviction, or sealed criminal record;
- ESTA or Visa Waiver Program entry;
- missed deadline, denial, NOID, RFE with material risk, or interview with adverse facts;
- prior petition, prior marriage, prior immigration filing, or prior visa refusal with inconsistencies;
- travel outside the United States while I-485 is pending;
- switching from adjustment of status to consular processing;
- any question where a wrong step could trigger a bar or loss of eligibility.

## Boundary

This sub-block is for research, organization, preparation, and monitoring. It does not replace individualized advice from a licensed immigration attorney.

## Final Rule

Verify current law and current agency practice separately. Do not confuse an internal USCIS memo, a media clarification, an attorney interpretation, and a forum anecdote.
