# US Law Legal Research Pipeline

## Purpose

Use this workflow to research United States legal issues safely, efficiently, and reproducibly.

## Core Workflow

```text
1. Capture the question in plain language.
2. Build the facts-and-dates table.
3. Identify urgency and possible deadlines before deep research.
4. Identify jurisdiction: federal, state, territory, county, city, court, agency, or contract-selected law.
5. Classify the legal issue: statute, regulation, procedure, case law, agency action, contract, or mixed issue.
6. Search primary sources first.
7. Use secondary sources and research platforms to locate authorities, explanations, and citations.
8. Verify every material proposition against current primary authority.
9. Check later amendments, effective dates, point-in-time versions, case treatment, local rules, and agency updates.
10. Separate confirmed law, interpretation, uncertainty, and practical options.
11. Decide whether attorney escalation is required.
12. Preserve the research package and source links.
```

## Facts And Dates First

Before analyzing, capture:

- who is involved;
- what happened;
- where it happened;
- when it happened;
- documents received or signed;
- notices, deadlines, hearing dates, filing dates, and response dates;
- agency, court, employer, landlord, business, or other counterparty;
- desired outcome;
- immediate risk if no action is taken.

## Jurisdiction First

Do not research abstractly when jurisdiction changes the answer.

Check:

- federal versus state law;
- which state or territory;
- county or municipal rules;
- court system and venue;
- agency-specific procedures;
- contract choice-of-law and forum clauses;
- immigration, tax, bankruptcy, intellectual-property, and other federal domains;
- tribal, military, or specialized jurisdiction when relevant.

## Deadline First

Before giving substantive analysis, check for:

- filing deadlines;
- appeal windows;
- response deadlines;
- hearing dates;
- statutes of limitation;
- administrative exhaustion deadlines;
- notice periods;
- cure periods;
- payment deadlines;
- immigration status or travel consequences;
- criminal or enforcement exposure.

If a deadline may exist and is not confirmed, state that clearly and escalate.

## Research Layers

### Layer 1 — Primary Authority

Use statutes, regulations, court opinions, rules, orders, agency decisions, and official forms or instructions.

### Layer 2 — Verification And Treatment

Check amendments, effective dates, point-in-time versions, later cases, citator treatment, local rules, standing orders, agency updates, and procedural notices.

### Layer 3 — Secondary Explanation

Use treatises, practice guides, legal-aid materials, bar guidance, law-review articles, and reputable legal analysis to clarify context and locate authorities.

### Layer 4 — Practical Path

Identify:

- immediate actions;
- documents to gather;
- questions for an attorney;
- options and tradeoffs;
- unresolved issues;
- escalation urgency.

## Output Format

A legal research memo should separate:

1. question presented;
2. facts relied on;
3. missing facts;
4. jurisdiction;
5. urgency and deadlines;
6. primary authorities;
7. analysis;
8. uncertainty and conflicting authority;
9. practical options;
10. attorney-escalation recommendation;
11. source list;
12. date checked.

## Final Rule

Research the controlling law, not the most convenient summary.
