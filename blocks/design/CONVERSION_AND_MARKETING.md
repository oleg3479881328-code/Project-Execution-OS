# Conversion and Marketing

## Purpose

Ensure website design supports business goals, not only visual taste.

## Core Rule

A beautiful page that does not move the visitor toward a decision is unfinished.

## Conversion Goal

Every page must define one primary conversion goal.

Examples:

- start trial;
- join waitlist;
- buy product;
- book call;
- download lead magnet;
- create account;
- request quote;
- read next article.

Secondary goals are allowed, but they must not compete with the primary goal.

## Message Hierarchy

A strong page answers in order:

1. What is this?
2. Who is it for?
3. What result do I get?
4. Why should I trust it?
5. What should I do now?

## CTA Rules

Good CTA:

- uses action language;
- matches the visitor's stage;
- appears at natural decision points;
- has enough surrounding context.

Bad CTA:

- vague;
- hidden;
- repeated without reason;
- promises more than the product can deliver.

## Objection Handling

Common objections:

- price;
- setup time;
- trust;
- privacy;
- cancellation;
- whether it works for my case;
- whether I need technical skill.

Address objections with sections, FAQ, proof, examples, and transparent limitations.

## Proof Ladder

From weaker to stronger:

1. product screenshots;
2. founder credibility;
3. early user quotes;
4. customer logos;
5. metrics;
6. case studies;
7. independent third-party proof.

Do not invent proof.

## Offer Design

A good offer clarifies:

- what is included;
- what happens first;
- what result is expected;
- what risk is reduced;
- what the user pays or commits.

## Page-Level Review

Ask:

- Is the audience clear?
- Is the promise concrete?
- Is the CTA visible?
- Is the next step low-friction?
- Is trust sufficient?
- Are objections answered?
- Is pricing understandable?
- Is the page too long or too thin for the decision?

## Final Rule

Conversion design is not manipulation. It is clarity, trust, and a clear next step.
